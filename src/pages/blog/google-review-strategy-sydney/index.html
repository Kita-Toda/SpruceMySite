A Google Review Strategy That Actually Works

Most Sydney businesses do brilliant work and have almost nothing to show for it on Google. Here's a system that fixes that without you having to beg.

C Christian Alba, SpruceMySite · 8 September 2026 · 2 min read

Here's an uncomfortable truth: the customers who love you almost never leave a review. The one who had a slightly off day with your invoice? They'll find the time. That imbalance is why so many genuinely great Sydney businesses sit on 4.3 stars with 11 reviews while a mediocre competitor with a slicker follow-up system sits on 4.8 with 200.

A review strategy isn't about being pushy. It's about closing the gap between how good you actually are and how good Google thinks you are — because for most local searches, Google's opinion is the only one a new customer sees.

Why Timing Beats Everything

The single biggest lever isn't the wording of your request. It's when you ask. Ask a customer for a review three weeks after the job's done and you're competing with their inbox, their week, and their memory of the details. Ask them within the hour — while the good feeling is still fresh — and you're not competing with anything.

The Receipt Moment The best time to ask is the moment a customer confirms they're happy: right after a job's signed off, a booking's completed, or a "thanks so much" text lands. That's the highest-trust window you'll ever get with them — and it closes fast.

How To Automate Google Review Requests Without Sounding Like A Robot

"Automated" and "impersonal" don't have to be the same thing. A good setup looks like this:

  • A trigger tied to a real moment — job marked complete, invoice paid, appointment finished — not a generic weekly blast.
  • A short SMS or email, from a person's name, with a direct link straight to your Google review box (not your homepage, not a Facebook page — the fewer clicks, the higher the completion rate).
  • One follow-up nudge a few days later for anyone who didn't click through, then it stops. No nagging.

This is exactly the kind of thing a CRM or automation platform (we run ours on GoHighLevel) handles quietly in the background — set up once, then it just runs while you're doing the actual job you're good at.

What A Real 5-Star Strategy Looks Like

Volume matters, but so does recency. Google — and customers — trust a business with 40 reviews spread over the last year more than one with 200 reviews that all landed in a single 2019 growth spurt and nothing since. Consistency beats a one-off push every time.

A realistic target for most Sydney small businesses is a handful of new reviews a month, forever, rather than a single "let's get reviews" campaign that fizzles out by March.

Handling The 1-Star Naysayer Like A Pro

You will get one eventually. It's not a strategy failure — it's proof the reviews are real. What matters is the reply: calm, specific, no defensiveness, and ideally an offer to sort it out offline. Future customers read your response to the bad review more closely than the review itself.
