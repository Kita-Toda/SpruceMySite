Is Your Website Actually Costing You Customers?

Traffic without enquiries is the most expensive kind of nothing. Here's how to tell whether your website is quietly working against you.

C Christian Alba, SpruceMySite · 8 September 2026 · 2 min read

A website that gets visitors but no enquiries isn't neutral — it's actively costing you. Every dollar spent on ads, every hour spent on SEO, every referral that clicks through and leaves without acting is money and effort going in and nothing coming back out.

The frustrating part is that most of the reasons are invisible unless you know exactly where to look.

7 Signs Worth Checking Right Now

  • No clear next step above the fold. If a visitor has to scroll and think before finding out how to contact you, most won't bother.
  • The phone number or booking link isn't clickable on mobile. Over half your traffic is likely on a phone. If they can't tap-to-call or tap-to-book in one move, you've lost them.
  • Slow load time. A site that takes more than a few seconds to load on mobile data loses a meaningful chunk of visitors before they even see your homepage.
  • No social proof near the decision point. Reviews, testimonials, or trust signals that only live on a separate "Testimonials" page rather than next to the button that matters.
  • Contact forms with too many required fields. Every extra field is another reason to abandon. Name, contact detail, one-line message — that's usually enough to start a conversation.
  • No follow-up after someone enquires. The site did its job getting the lead — but if nobody replies for two days, the lead's already gone elsewhere.
  • Generic copy that could belong to any business in Australia. If nothing on the page is specific to Sydney, your suburb, or your actual customer's problem, there's no reason to trust you over the next search result.

How To Check Your Own Conversion Rate

Rough and simple: take the number of enquiries (calls, form fills, bookings) over a month, divide by the number of visitors to your site in that same month, and multiply by 100. That's your conversion rate. There's no single "correct" number — it varies wildly by industry — but if you can see the trend moving down, or you genuinely have no idea what the number is, that's the real problem to fix first.

Traffic Problem Or Conversion Problem?

If you're getting very few visitors at all, more content and SEO is the right first move. If you're getting decent traffic but almost no enquiries, more traffic won't fix it — the site itself needs work. Getting this diagnosis wrong is the single most common reason marketing budget gets wasted.
